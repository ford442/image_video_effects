# New Shader Plan: Liquid-Metal Cymatic Resonator

## Overview
A hyper-reflective pool of ferromagnetic liquid that dances and crystallizes into complex geometric mandala patterns driven by simulated acoustic resonances.

## Features
- Dynamic ray-marched liquid metal surface with high-gloss specular reflections
- Real-time cymatic pattern generation based on interference of traveling standing waves
- Mouse interaction acts as a physical perturbation, sending gravity ripples across the metallic pool
- Audio-reactive frequency sampling that drives the amplitude and complexity of the geometric nodes
- Iridescent sub-surface scattering that shifts colors at grazing angles
- Temporal accumulation for motion blur and viscous drag effects

## Technical Implementation
- File: public/shaders/gen-liquid-metal-cymatic-resonator.wgsl
- Category: generative
- Tags: ["raymarching", "cymatics", "liquid-metal", "audio-reactive", "reflections"]
- Algorithm: A ray-marched heightfield driven by summing multiple sine waves (Fourier series) in polar coordinates, creating complex interference patterns, rendered with a metallic physically-based shading model.

### Core Algorithm
The core uses a 2D heightmap generated by summing multiple wave functions in polar coordinates (radius, angle). By combining high-frequency sine waves with time-varying phase shifts, intricate Chladni-like patterns emerge. The heightmap is ray-marched as a 3D terrain. Normals are calculated via finite differences of the heightfield for accurate lighting.

### Mouse Interaction
The mouse acts as an attractive/repulsive gravity well. The distance from the mouse `(mouse_uv)` to the surface point heavily modulates the amplitude and phase of the local cymatic waves, creating an interactive disturbance that propagates outward like a shockwave.

### Color Mapping / Shading
The shading relies heavily on environment mapping (simulated via gradient maps or procedural skies) reflected off the calculated normals. Metallic material properties are simulated using the Schlick approximation for Fresnel. Grazing angles exhibit an iridescent, multi-color sheen (thin-film interference), while direct reflections are crisp and silver/chrome.
