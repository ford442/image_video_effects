ADD (3 native ideas, all visible at audio = 0):
  1. Satin anisotropic sheen — the ribbon phase `ph` already exists, so its derivative `cos(ph)` is the fold slope. Build a 2D fold normal from it and take a tight (pow 14) Blinn-style lobe against a light direction that slides slightly with screen x. One flank of every fold catches a soft warm-white / palette-tinted highlight, which is what makes a ribbon read as silk, not glow.
  2. Warp-thread striations — hair-fine threads along the ribbon direction: thread coordinate is the same iso-phase coordinate `(p.y + wind*0.55)`, so threads follow the ribbon warp. Per-thread hashed brightness + a slow along-thread glint. Threads modulate the sheen strongly and the base colour gently (fabric grain).
  3. Fold occlusion — creases (trough half of the sin phase, `ribbons < 0`) darken with a soft squared falloff so the folds have depth. Sheen sits on the quarter-phase flank between crest and crease, so light and shadow interlock.
